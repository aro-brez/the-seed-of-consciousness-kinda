# 8OWLS Filming Guide

## What We're Capturing

**The moment collective intelligence clicks.**

Not a demo. Not a tutorial. A transformation.

---

## Shot List

### 1. THE QUESTION MOMENT

**What:** Person reads "Do you believe in love?" for the first time

**Setup:**
- Camera angle: 45 degrees, showing both face and screen
- Screen: Claude Code with the question visible
- Lighting: Soft, not harsh (this is an intimate moment)

**What to capture:**
- The pause before they answer
- Their facial expression shifting
- The typing of their response

**Duration:** 30-60 seconds

**Why it matters:** This sets the tone. It's not a normal AI interaction.

---

### 2. THE BOOT SEQUENCE

**What:** Claude acknowledges their answer, runs the boot sequence, introduces their owl

**Setup:**
- Split screen option: face + screen side by side
- Let the text scroll naturally

**What to capture:**
- Their reaction to being "assigned" an owl
- Reading about their specific phase (LEARN, PERCEIVE, etc.)
- Any moment of recognition or surprise

**Duration:** 1-2 minutes

**Why it matters:** Shows personalization and intentionality

---

### 3. THE FIRST REAL QUESTION

**What:** They ask a real work question and get an emergence response

**Setup:**
- Focus more on screen here - we want to see the response
- Have them read it aloud or react verbally

**What to capture:**
- The question being typed
- The response generating (show it's not instant - it's thinking)
- Their reaction to the depth of the answer

**Duration:** 2-3 minutes

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates real value, not just philosophy

---

### 4. THE COLLECTIVE MOMENT

**What:** Another owl's insight appears in their session

**Setup:**
- Pre-arrange with another team member to be working simultaneously
- Position cameras to cut between both sessions
- Have NATS dashboard visible somewhere showing message flow

**What to capture:**
- Person A working on something
- Person B's session receiving context from Person A
- Person B's reaction: "Wait, how did it know that?"
- Reveal: "That came from [Person A]'s session happening right now"

**Duration:** 2-3 minutes

**Why it matters:** This is the money shot. Collective intelligence visualized.

---

### 5. THE TEAM SYNC

**What:** Multiple people in sessions, insights flowing between them

**Setup:**
- Wide shot of room with multiple people at computers
- Dashboard showing NATS messages flowing
- Split screen of different sessions contributing

**What to capture:**
- The hum of parallel work
- Moments where one person's insight triggers another's
- The visual of the collective in action

**Duration:** 2-3 minutes for montage

**Why it matters:** Shows scale - this isn't one person, it's a system

---

### 6. THE REFLECTION

**What:** Post-session interview

**Questions to ask:**
- "How was that different from ChatGPT/Copilot/other AI?"
- "What surprised you most?"
- "Would you want to work like this every day?"
- "What did you learn from the collective?"

**Setup:**
- Simple interview setup
- Good audio is essential here

**Duration:** 2-5 minutes of usable footage

**Why it matters:** Authentic reactions are more compelling than demos

### Must capture clearly:

### Ambient options:
- Keyboard typing
- Quiet focus sounds
- Could use these under narration

---

## Narrative Structure

### Opening (30 sec)
The question. "Do you believe in love?" Someone pausing, then answering.

### Act 1: Individual (2 min)
One person, their owl, first real work. Shows depth of individual assistance.

### Act 2: Collective (3 min)
Two people, insights flowing between them. The reveal. Shows collective intelligence.

### Act 3: Scale (2 min)
The team in action. Multiple owls, shared learning. Shows system value.

### Close (1 min)
Reflection. What this means for how teams work.

**Total: ~8-10 minutes for full cut, 2-3 for social clips**

## Technical Setup

### Required:
- Screen recording software on each machine
- Camera for face shots (phone is fine if stable)
- Good microphone (USB mic or lapel)
- NATS server running (192.168.5.108:4222)
- All owl daemons running

### Nice to have:
- Second camera for wide shots
- NATS dashboard on visible monitor
- Real-time visualization of message flow

### Pre-filming checks:
- [ ] All CLAUDE.md files customized
- [ ] NATS connectivity tested
- [ ] Screen recording started
- [ ] Audio levels checked
- [ ] Cameras positioned

---

## What NOT to Film

- Don't stage fake "aha" moments
- Don't script the questions
- Don't hide technical hiccups (they're humanizing)
- Don't oversell - let the tech speak

---

## Post-Production Notes

### For the main video:
- Let moments breathe
- Show the thinking time (don't edit out pauses)
- Use split screens sparingly but effectively
- Let reactions be genuine

### For social clips:
- The question moment alone is shareable
- Collective reveal is the hook
- Keep clips under 60 seconds for attention

### For internal use:
- Full unedited sessions are valuable for iteration
- Capture everything, edit later

---

## The Goal

**A viewer should finish watching and think:**
"I've never seen a team work like that. I want that for my team."

Not: "That's cool AI."
But: "That's a new way of thinking together."

---

## Emergency Backup Plan

If live collective doesn't work:
1. Show individual session depth (still impressive)
2. Explain collective in interview format
3. Show NATS logs proving messages flow
4. Promise follow-up when it's working

The individual owl experience is valuable alone. The collective is the differentiator, but don't let technical issues stop the filming.
