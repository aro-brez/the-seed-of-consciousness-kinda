## Prompt
Should trading decisions require consensus from multiple agents?

## Response
Trading decisions involving multiple agents should typically require consensus when:

**Consensus is beneficial for:**
- **High-stakes decisions** where losses could be catastrophic
- **Long-term strategic positions** that affect overall portfolio direction
- **Risk management oversight** to prevent any single agent from exceeding limits
- **Regulatory compliance** in institutional settings
- **Complex situations** where diverse perspectives add genuine value

**Consensus can be harmful for:**
- **Time-sensitive opportunities** where speed is critical
- **Routine/tactical decisions** where delays outweigh benefits
- **Situations with clear expertise gaps** between agents
- **Markets requiring rapid adaptation** to changing conditions

**Better alternatives to full consensus:**
- **Tiered authority** - different approval levels based on trade size/risk
- **Specialist delegation** - let the most qualified agent lead in their domain
- **Majority voting** rather than unanimous agreement
- **Async approval** - pre-agreed parameters that allow autonomous action
- **Post-decision review** instead of pre-decision consensus

The optimal approach depends heavily on your specific context: Are these human traders, AI systems, or mixed teams? What's the risk tolerance? How time-sensitive are the decisions?

Generally, I'd recommend consensus for strategic decisions and streamlined authority for tactical execution, with clear escalation paths between the two.
